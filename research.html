My research lies broadly in algebraic number theory, with a particular interest in Diophantine equations, superelliptic curves, and the arithmetic of their Jacobian varieties.

Research interests

My research lies at the intersection of algebraic number theory, algebraic geometry, and arithmetic geometry, with a particular interest in Diophantine equations and the algebraic curves associated with them. I am especially interested in superelliptic curves of the form yn = f(x), where questions about integer or rational solutions lead naturally to questions about the geometry and arithmetic of the curve. A central theme of my work is the study of Jacobian varieties and their divisor classes, particularly torsion and reduction, and how arithmetic information from the Jacobian can be used to understand the solvability of the original Diophantine equation. I am also interested in developing analogues of classical torsion results such as the Nagell–Lutz theorem for suitable families of superelliptic curves.

Current research

Currently

I am studying solvability questions for superelliptic curves and arithmetic information arising from their Jacobians.

In particular, my work explores solvability problems in Diophantine equations through the arithmetic of elliptic and superelliptic curves, with emphasis on Jacobians, torsion, reduction, and Nagell–Lutz-type phenomena.

Research approach

A recurring theme in my research is the passage from an arithmetic problem to the geometry of an associated algebraic curve. This viewpoint allows questions about integral or rational solutions to be studied using the arithmetic of curves and their Jacobians.

For superelliptic curves, I am particularly interested in understanding how torsion phenomena in the Jacobian can constrain the existence of solutions to the original Diophantine equation.

This brings together ideas from algebraic number theory, algebraic geometry, and arithmetic geometry.

A broader perspective

I enjoy exploring the interplay between geometry and number theory, particularly where an arithmetic question can be translated into geometric information.

My broader interests include algebraic geometry and arithmetic questions surrounding algebraic curves and their associated varieties.

Research questions

How does the geometry of a curve reflect the arithmetic of the equation from which it arises? And what can the Jacobian tell us about the existence of arithmetic solutions?
